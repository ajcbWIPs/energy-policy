US Residential Rooftop Solar and Battery Adoption

State-level view of the three main economic drivers of residential PV uptake: household disposable income, grid electricity bills and annual PV energy yield. Payback and an adoption propensity index are modelled from these inputs and compared against observed penetration.

System and economic assumptions

Defaults reflect cash purchase in 2026 with no federal homeowner credit. Third-party leases and PPAs can still pass through the 48E credit, so set the incentive slider near 30% to approximate that pathway.

Method and sources

Electricity price and consumption. EIA Form 861 / Electric Power Monthly, 2024 annual residential averages (cents per kWh and kWh per customer per month). Annual bill is price times consumption.

Disposable income. BEA per capita disposable personal income by state (SAINC51), 2024, rounded. Household figure is per capita times the persons per household slider.

Annual PV yield. Specific yield in kWh per kWp per year for a fixed, south facing roof array at about 20 degree tilt with 14% system losses, representative of NREL PVWatts runs at major population centres. Real sites vary by roughly 10% with orientation and shading.

Observed adoption. Penetration is an indicative estimate of the share of households with rooftop PV, derived from cumulative residential system counts (LBNL Tracking the Sun, SEIA/Wood Mackenzie) over ACS household counts. Battery attachment is the indicative share of new residential PV systems installed with storage. Both are rounded estimates and should be replaced with your own pulls before drawing conclusions.

Savings model. Year one generation is size times yield. Self-consumed energy (capped at household load) is valued at the retail rate and exports at the export credit fraction. Lifetime value is 25 years of savings with retail escalation, 0.5% per year module degradation and discounting. Payback is simple (net cost over year one savings). Battery value here is bill arbitrage only and excludes backup value, VPP payments and time-of-use spreads, so it understates battery economics in CA and HI.

Adoption propensity index. Weighted z-scores across states: payback (40%, inverted), bill burden as share of household disposable income (30%) and disposable income (30%), rescaled 0 to 100. The scatter tests how well each driver explains log penetration.

Replacing the data. Place a file at data/states.csv next to this page with the same columns as the Export CSV output (code, price, kwh_mo, dpi, yield, pen, attach). Any column present overrides the embedded value on load.
