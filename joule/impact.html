Why it matters

Every litre you save keeps food moving and the lights on.

Australia imports nearly all of its diesel and holds only weeks of it. The same tank of fuel that moves your car competes with the trucks that stock supermarkets and the generators that power remote towns. Here is what your choices are connected to.

Food on the shelf

Almost everything you eat rode on diesel

Food is grown with diesel tractors and pumps, then trucked between farms, processors, distribution centres and stores, often thousands of kilometres. When diesel prices jump, freight costs rise and those costs reach the checkout. The further a town is from a capital, the bigger that share of the price.

Power in the bush

Hundreds of communities make their electricity from diesel

Between 200 and 300 remote communities, and more than 100 mines, run on diesel generators because they are too far from the grid. That diesel arrives by truck or barge from the same national stock. In a shortage, keeping those generators fed means keeping hospitals, water pumps, cold stores and schools running.

One Step Off the Grid
The long-term fix is solar and batteries in those towns. Remote hybrids already cost less than diesel power, and the diesel framework sets a target of at least 70% renewable power in every diesel-dependent community by 2030. Until then, every litre the rest of the country doesn't burn is a litre that can reach them.
