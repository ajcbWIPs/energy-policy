Working in Australia today

1. Battery-swap trucks on the freight highways

Electric prime movers swap a flat pack for a full one in about 4 minutes at hubs beside solar and wind farms, instead of stopping to charge.

Each truck: 78,300 km a year at 53 L/100 km (ABS), about 41,600 L. Running cost A$0.30/km electric against A$1.03/km diesel on the Hume (RenewEconomy, before this year's price rise). Corridor lengths: Hume 880 km, Newell 1,060 km, Pacific 920 km, Bruce 1,680 km. The framework's truck package budgets about A$4 bn for 40,000 trucks. 4. Solar and batteries for remote communities

Diesel generators power hundreds of towns too far from the grid. Solar alone cuts about 15% of their diesel; solar with enough battery storage cuts 50 to 96%.

No national figure for diesel burned for power is published, so the average per community is your guess. Maningrida, one of the largest, uses up to 10,000 L a day. Real results: 25 NT communities cut diesel about 15% with 10 MW of solar; Mount Weld mine ran 95.7% renewable with wind, solar and batteries.

Open questions

Where research time and energy would pay off most

These are the gaps that hold the hypotheticals back. Each one is a good project for a student team, a lab or a startup.

  1. Halophyte yields in the field. No Australian site has yet shown commercial seed-oil yields at scale. Replicated trials on inland scalds and coastal flats would settle whether 220 or 730 L/ha is realistic.
  2. Fertiliser from fish farms. Australia imports about 95% of its urea, 65 to 70% of it from the Gulf. Can nitrogen from land-based aquaculture ponds feed halophytes and crops instead?
  3. Lighter packs for farm machinery. A 600 kWh combine pack weighs about 3.5 to 4 tonnes today. Higher energy density, or swapping smaller packs more often, would make AgSwap practical.
  4. One swap standard for Australia. Trucks, buses and farm machines from different makers need packs that fit the same stations, as CATL's standard does in China.
  5. Batteries in outback heat and dust. Cooling, fire safety and lifespan at 45°C on the Stuart or Great Northern highways.
  6. Grid connections for hubs. Sharing a solar or wind farm's connection could skip the slowest step in building a charging hub. The rules and engineering need testing.
  7. How much diesel makes electricity? No national figure is published. A generator audit would size the cheapest diesel to remove.
  8. Recycling and refining battery minerals here. Batteries swap a fuel dependency for a minerals one. Recycling packs and processing lithium onshore keeps that value and security in Australia.
